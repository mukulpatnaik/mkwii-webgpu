In-game controls ideas

1 + 2 · Combined1 · HOME Menu2 · Player bar3 · Shelf

Three ways to lose the sidebar. In all of them the game picture takes the whole window and nothing is on screen until you ask for it. The Solo / In a room switch at the top shows what changes online: the room shows up, and the game cannot pause.

On pausing. The page cannot pause the game today; it can only set volume, mute, rebind a key and name the player. For a solo race, pausing when a menu opens is a small addition to the runtime (hold the frame loop and the audio while the menu is up). In a room it must keep running: an online race has no pause, and freezing one browser would drop that player. So the modal options below say "Game paused" in solo and "The race keeps going" in a room.

1 + 2 · Combined a faint corner button that opens into the slim pill

While you race there is one faint round button in the top right. It fades out completely after about three seconds without mouse movement and comes back when the mouse moves. Click it and it stretches leftward into the pill: Controls, Saves, the volume, Help and full screen slide out of it, and the button itself turns into the close mark. Controls, Saves and Help drop a small card under the pill; the game keeps running behind it. Click the close mark, or press Esc, and the pill folds back into the button.

Try it: move the mouse over the picture, click the round button, then Controls. Hold the mouse still to watch it fade.

1stLap 2 / 3
  • On screen while playing. Nothing when the mouse is still. One faint button when it moves. The open pill stays until you close it, so it never vanishes mid-click.
  • Pause. None: the cards are small and the race stays visible, so solo and a room behave the same and the runtime needs no change. A solo pause can still be added later for the cards.
  • Keyboard and pad. Esc opens and closes the pill, since those players never move the mouse. In a room the pill gains the room count, which opens the roster and invite.
  • Remapping keys. The Controls card lists the keys and takes clicks to rebind, as the sidebar does; the full settings stay on F10.

1 · The HOME Menu one button, then a full overlay; pauses in solo

What the Wii itself does when you press HOME mid-game: the picture dims between two black bars. A small faded button sits in the corner, and Esc does the same. The bars hold the title, the state and the volume; the middle holds three big buttons that open Controls, Saves and Help as a card. The most Wii of the three, and the only one where pausing is natural.

Try it: click the HOME button in the corner, then a big button.

2 · The player bar a video-player strip that fades; nothing modal, no pause

A slim pill at the top of the picture, like a video player's controls: it shows when the mouse moves and fades after a few seconds. Volume is right there; Controls, Room and Help open a small card under the bar and the game keeps running behind it. The least interruption, and the same in solo and online.

Try it: move the mouse over the picture, then click Controls or Help.

1stLap 2 / 3
  • On screen while playing. Nothing after a few seconds without the mouse. Keyboard and pad players never see it unless they reach for the mouse.
  • Pause. None, by design: the cards are small and the race stays visible. Nothing differs between solo and a room.
  • Cost. The smallest runtime change (none). The bindings list has to fit a compact card, so remapping keeps its own bigger view or stays in F10.

3 · The shelf the menu's curved bar, tucked under the picture

The same curved bar as the menu before the game, hidden below the bottom edge with a small tab showing. Click the tab (or press Esc) and it slides up with the same round buttons, Saves, Controls and Help, plus the volume in its bay; a round button opens its card over the dimmed game. One vocabulary before and during the game.

Try it: click the Menu tab at the bottom, then a round button.

  • On screen while playing. A small tab at the bottom edge, faded. The raised shelf covers the bottom third of the picture; the cards cover the middle.
  • Pause. Optional: the shelf alone can leave the race running (it is only a strip), and a card can pause in solo as the HOME Menu does.
  • Cost. Reuses the menu's bar, round buttons and cards almost as they are, so the least new styling.

How they compare

  • Most minimal while racing: 2, then 1, then 3.
  • Most Wii: 1. It is the console's own answer to this exact problem.
  • Most consistent with the new menu: 3, since it is the same bar.
  • Best for remapping keys: 1 and 3, which have room for the full bindings list. 2 keeps cards small.
  • A mix that works: 1 for the menu, plus only the volume and mute from 2 as a fading corner control.
